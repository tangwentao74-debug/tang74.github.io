Procter & Gamble challenged our team to reimagine the packaging experience for one of their consumer products, and to deliver a final recommendation and a working prototype in ten weeks.

I worked across the whole project — from desk research and in-home visits, to planning and moderating our concept tests, to prototyping and refining our final concept and presenting it to P&G. What I care about is turning what people actually do into decisions a team can build on.

This project is under NDA, so specific details have been omitted. This page is about how I worked, not what we found.

01

What does the real routine look like?

Secondary research · In-home visits · Observation · Affinity mapping · Persona · Journey mapping
STARTING AT A DESK
Before going into anyone’s home, I did our secondary research — existing packaging, competitors, materials and manufacturing constraints — and split it into what P&G already knew and what was still open. With ten weeks, only the open questions were worth taking into the field.
IN THE HOME
I ran two in-home visits. I asked people to walk me through their routine in their own space, step by step, instead of describing it.
MAKING SENSE OF IT
After the visits, we clustered our notes into an affinity map, shaped a persona around the patterns, and turned what we saw into a short list of design requirements to test next.
THE JOURNEY MAP I LED
Each of us had mapped our own visits. I led the work of merging four maps into one end-to-end journey the whole team could design around.

02

How do you get people to show, not tell?

Test planning · Stimulus design · Prototyping · Moderation
ROUND 1 · THE CRITIQUE
Our first plan compared prototypes almost at random. Our professor pushed back: if every prototype changes five things, you can’t tell what people are reacting to.
ROUND 1 · WHAT I BUILT AND RAN
I planned the session and built prototypes that each changed one variable — 17 in all, painted white with the branding covered. A token trade-off and a build-your-own kit made choices visible. I moderated two of the five sessions.
AFTER EACH ROUND
We sorted the notes, checked every claim against what people actually did, and turned what was still open into the plan for the next round.
